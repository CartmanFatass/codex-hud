B10C performance route — Oracle decision, 2026-11-02

**Choose option 1: one owned Node worker thread per long-lived HUD process, containing the existing delegation collection pipeline. Keep B12’s per-session lease as the sole publication authority. Do not introduce a shared collector daemon or a combined lease/publish API. Defer the broad B9 split in option 3.**

This is a design decision for owner approval, not acceptance of the existing implementation or authorization to implement it. It requires the contract-06 amendments identified below. Until those amendments are approved, the earlier awaited, same-operation `afterPaint` ruling and the existing full-tick gate remain binding, and the current changing populations remain failed.

The evidence supports isolating synchronous work, not a claim about its kernel cause. The final matched bar maximum is 3590.488 ms, including 3557.670 ms of lease maintenance before the publication tail. The panel maximum is 568.279 ms, including 463.522 ms in `publishPrepared`. Moving only the tail cannot remove the former. The raw changing B9 distributions are p99/max 29.888/37.964 ms for bar and 29.644/34.568 ms for panel. These findings come from the report (`tmp/tasks/B10C/report.md`, not in git), raw samples (`perf-after-readhead.json`), and matched stage samples (`perf-stage-samples.json`); the earlier failed runs remain evidence.

1. **Execution route, ownership, and bounds**

Move `HudDelegationCollector`’s complete production observation operation into the worker: source/store startup and identity replacement, ledger reads, existing fair reader/topology selection, lease acquisition/renewal/release, recovery, B16 preparation/merge/publication, committed-head reads/decode, and existing B9/view-model assembly. The worker returns a detached read model. The main thread owns interaction, display state, strings, frame-diff, and paint enqueue.

This is a relocation of the existing collector, not a second collector alongside it. The main thread must not retain another delegation reader/store/writer or synchronously read the observation database when painting. Moving the entire `HudDelegationCollector` boundary also avoids introducing a new round-trip protocol between its tightly coupled scheduler, publisher and B9 inputs. Existing unrelated/legacy HUD collection is not implicitly moved or duplicated; its work still counts in the paint measurement when it executes there.

Use one worker for all sessions handled by a given long-lived bar or panel. Two live HUD processes may have two workers; they still have at most one accepted publisher for any full `SessionRef`. A losing worker reads committed projections and does not open a publication reader merely to prepare work it cannot publish. Short-lived statusline/status/wait calls do not start a worker. With no existing valid HUD home/ledger, preserve the legacy path and start zero observation workers.

Within the worker, retain the acquiring `ObservationStore` instance, its `StatusWriter`, the real reader, prepared tokens and lazy recovery lookups together. `ObservationStore` has instance-local authority, and prepared checkpoints/status readings use identity-backed maps (`observation-store.ts:136`, `checkpoint.ts:1094`, `status/writer.ts:49`). A cloned lease or prepared token cannot confer authority in another thread. None of those objects crosses IPC.

Keep opportunistic leases: default TTL 15 seconds; renewal no more frequently than 5 seconds and only on selected work that needs authority. Do not renew the display list. Expiry, fencing, source/run/binding changes and restart retain the earlier discard/recovery rules. SQLite WAL + FULL, the final transaction fence/CAS, and commit-before-reader-ACK remain unchanged. Intents remain ledger projections with no collect lease or fake observation commit.

Scheduling is bounded as follows:

- The existing HUD collection cadence requests work. There is one active worker operation and at most one coalesced pending request per HUD. Replacing a pending request preserves its oldest enqueue time for latency accounting. No per-session worker, unbounded event FIFO, renewal timer, flusher or independent catch-up loop is introduced.
- The worker retains the existing fair cursors, one normal reader grant, and at most one selected publication per collection operation. Watchers invalidate/coalesce work; they do not create another publication stream. Missed normal opportunities may leave one pending request, not a backlog of identical ticks.
- There is at most one unconsumed result. Deliver and acknowledge that result before another publication can produce another result. This transport acknowledgement is separate from the reader/recovery ACK, which remains exclusively tied to durable commit. Do not use latest-frame coalescing to discard unconsumed committed notices or publication outcomes.
- Preserve the shared 8 MiB collection allowance and cooperative deadline across all existing participants, including the legacy root read. Allocate grants once for the logical collection operation; extra paints and requests while a worker is busy do not create fresh reader budgets. Do not replace the shared allowance with an independent 8 MiB allowance on each thread. B12M’s accounting gap remains applicable.

The IPC result is a versioned, bounded, plain-data `DelegationViewModel` envelope: worker incarnation, scope/request identity, monotonic view revision, actual ledger boundary/observation time, original per-session observation stamps, coverage/continuation/health, and timing/accounting results. It contains the existing tree/details needed by the renderer; it contains no reader, checkpoint body, recovery index, full transcript, lease authority or callable handle. An unchanged result is a small acknowledgement, not another full-tree clone.

The main thread rejects obsolete worker incarnations and scope epochs, adopts one complete compatible result, and paints it without waiting for another worker request. A worker-local view revision is a transport sequence, not a new observation sequence or ledger high-water. Session summaries remain committed-only; intents and other already-permitted ledger projections keep their existing provenance. An assembled view is not a new cross-store atomicity guarantee: preserve separate ledger and observation stamps and all run/source compatibility checks.

A slow or unavailable worker leaves the last complete view available. Paint-side clocks and freshness use the existing pure freshness logic and the captured deadlines, never message receipt time. In particular, extract/reuse the pure part of `LinkTickCollector.availableFreshness`; do not create a second state classifier. Crossing a deadline may make the displayed source stale without writing a notice or checkpoint. A newly received old snapshot cannot become fresh merely because it arrived now. Before any compatible commit exists, retain the existing unavailable/initializing behavior. A scope change must not display an old scope’s view as the new one.

The worker’s owner is the `HudDelegationCollector` client in its HUD process. Start it once during actual production startup; retain it only for that collector’s lifetime. On close, stop accepting requests, invalidate the client epoch, and ask it to finish/settle its current operation, release valid leases best-effort and close its readers/stores. Give graceful cleanup a **2-second waiting allowance**, then stop waiting and use the owning HUD’s termination path. Do not keep the HUD alive indefinitely to release leases; expiry handles abandoned authority. Record dirty shutdown and close/checkpoint costs. A native SQLite stall is not safely cancelled by a JavaScript timeout, and termination during an unresolved operation is a crash-recovery case, not proof that it rolled back.

A worker cannot outlive its process. On an unexpected worker exit, keep the UI available, report degradation, and allow at most one automatic replacement during that HUD lifetime, on an ordinary subsequent collection opportunity and only after the old worker has actually exited. A stalled worker is not an excuse to start a concurrent replacement. Later failures require a new owner lifecycle; no respawn loop. Restart obtains fresh authority and reconciles durable state before further publication.

For the 200-node/20,000-header supported profile, impose these **new B10C resource budgets**, to be validated rather than presented as measured facts:

| Resource | Limit / acceptance |
|---|---|
| Observation workers | At most one per participating HUD; zero independent service processes; zero for legacy/no-ledger and short-lived read-only entries |
| Requests/results | One active operation, one pending coalesced request, one unconsumed result |
| Transport | At most 2 MiB encoded display payload per result; retain the adopted view plus at most one incoming view on the paint side |
| Resident native readers | At most 16; visible committed node metadata remains bounded by the 200-node scope; remove obsolete-scope caches |
| Worker JS guard | `maxOldGenerationSizeMb: 64`, `maxYoungGenerationSizeMb: 8`, `stackSizeMb: 4`; verify effective limits |
| Owned external buffers | At most 24 MiB in aggregate, including live incomplete-record buffers, read buffers and transfer buffers; SQLite/native allocation is additionally covered by process-memory measurement |
| Additional process RSS versus the same synchronous collector/profile | No more than 32 MiB steady after warmup, or 64 MiB peak including startup; report each HUD and the simultaneous aggregate |

Evict only quiescent, accepted reader state that can be recovered correctly; keep enough uninterrupted continuation to consume a maximum legal record. Never discard exact IDs, open calls, unacknowledged evidence or notice history to satisfy memory targets. Bound/admit new readers before allocation, rather than discovering the limit only after creating 200 transient buffers. A legal workload that cannot meet the limits is a failed resource qualification requiring another decision, not permission to silently truncate semantics or raise the ceilings. The transport limit likewise does not authorize removing required public fields; existing explicit coverage/truncation rules apply and the supported maximum fixture must still work.

Node’s worker `resourceLimits` constrain the JS engine, not ArrayBuffers or total RSS, and global OOM can still abort the process. Measure whole-process RSS against a matched baseline; do not sum identical process-wide RSS readings from its threads. These limits are not a native-memory sandbox. [Node worker documentation](https://nodejs.org/docs/latest-v24.x/api/worker_threads.html#new-workerfilename-options)

Reject a shared out-of-process collector for this package: it adds discovery, authentication/IPC, cross-client lifetime and owner-transfer rules, and a new resident process. It is not needed for single-publisher correctness because B12 already supplies that boundary. Reject a worker per session, and reject retaining the synchronous pre-paint acquire path. A shared service can be a later product-level decision if measured many-HUD memory warrants it; it is not a fallback silently enabled here.

2. **Measurement boundary and latency budgets**

**[CONTRACT-06 CHANGE] Replace the single completion gate with separately named responsiveness and observation-progress gates.** This explicitly changes H03, P-06-7, “增量 tick 验收”, and the corresponding design §6 wording. It is not compliant with the old contract merely because SQLite moved to another thread.

Use one monotonic trace for each operation, with request, worker-start, durable-commit-return, snapshot-post, adoption and paint-enqueue timestamps. Correlate clocks with a documented conservative error bound when measurements cross contexts/processes. Keep the original full-operation interval available as an end-to-end series; never calculate it by adding independent percentiles.

| Metric | Boundary | New gate |
|---|---|---|
| Paint responsiveness | From the scheduled paint becoming runnable to its final frame enqueue. For a worker-result paint, begin at result posting, before message serialization/delivery; include main-thread queue delay, adoption/validation, any paint-side decode, freshness/clock work, existing main-thread collection on that path, strings and frame-diff. | Empirical nearest-rank p99 **< 50 ms**, observed max **< 200 ms**; retain the 1,000-sample rolling rule of at most 10 samples ≥50 ms. |
| Selected incremental observation operation | From the HUD enqueueing that collection request to the corresponding complete committed view being enqueued for paint. Include time behind an active request, reader/lease/recovery, B9/B16, all SQLite and checkpoint/open/close work on the path, serialization/IPC and paint. | p99 **< 100 ms**, max **< 1,000 ms**; every 1,000-sample window has at most 10 samples ≥100 ms. A required update that has not committed is not a successful old-frame sample. |
| Local publisher commit-to-paint | From successful durable COMMIT return to enqueueing a view containing that commit or a compatible newer commit. Include post-commit assembly, IPC and scheduling. | p99 **< 50 ms**, max **< 200 ms**. Do not wait for the next 1/5-second collection poll to adopt a ready result. |

These are measured qualification limits, not promises that the OS can preempt synchronous disk I/O. The 100/1,000 ms progress budget is a proposed product change: normal selected work should occupy a small fraction of the existing 1-second active cadence and should not routinely lose a whole cadence. It is not derived by rounding up the observed worst sample. **The existing 3.59-second bar sample would still fail the new observation-progress gate.** Worker isolation removes its direct UI blocking; it does not cure its storage latency. If such stalls recur after relocation, that storage/concurrency profile remains performance-unqualified until separately resolved. Do not claim that this route alone guarantees acceptance.

Also report request→commit and append/change→first corresponding display. Commit-to-paint by itself excludes the expensive commit and cannot stand in for publication latency. Retain failed, busy, continued, superseded and incomplete operation records and the time to subsequent successful progress. Coalescing a request cannot reset its waiting clock or turn a required update into a no-op.

Followers have an additional discovery interval. The local publisher’s sub-200 ms post-commit rule is not a false promise of sub-200 ms propagation to an idle separate HUD. Retain the existing maximum normal collection interval `C` (normally 1 second active / 5 seconds idle); projection invalidation hints may make discovery earlier but are not a reliable guarantee. For a follower’s externally committed, compatible, in-scope head, measure actual commit→paint and require p99 **< C + 100 ms**, max **< C + 1,000 ms** on the supported healthy profile. Once its worker posts that view, the same paint gate applies. Do not add 100/200 ms database polling to manufacture a better propagation number. Missed hints, an already-busy worker, read-only query cost and the normal fallback must be represented in these measurements.

The selected-operation gate is not a promise to refresh 200 simultaneously dirty sources in one second. Keep the established single-grant fairness model. Report per-session oldest eligible age, grants, continuation, last successful observation and backlog/coverage. For a fixed ready set, a continuously eligible session must receive its next fair opportunity within one full traversal of that set; a successful but obsolete-frame response is not progress. Large-record continuation must receive a sufficient legal grant rather than repeated small grants. Measure the actual burst-drain time. Increasing publication throughput or promising a new whole-tree freshness SLA would be another scheduling decision, not an interpretation of these per-operation budgets.

Startup, true cold catch-up and shutdown remain separately named before measurement. Ordinary lease expiry, acquisition, busy recovery or source replacement during steady operation stay in their incremental populations. Startup must show initializing and measure launch→first consistent paint; the existing `openingMs` includes fixture creation and is not that measurement. Terminal physical display completion is still outside the paint-enqueue endpoint.

**[DESIGN §6 CHANGE/CLARIFICATION, not an existing pass]** Define no-change wiring’s ≤2 ms share as **p99 ≤2 ms**, including main-thread request/result bookkeeping and adoption checks; report its maximum and retain the overall paint maximum. The present evidence does not satisfy an every-sample ≤2 ms interpretation. Preserve the other stage shares: B9 p99 ≤30/max ≤40 ms; compact 200×56 row construction/frame-diff ≤10 ms; the existing rich/render totals remain applicable. The stage shares do not substitute for any complete-operation gate.

3. **B9 optimization decision**

**Option 3 is not required now.** The current B9 distributions already meet the explicit 30/40 ms share; the no-publisher baseline’s changing full p99 is 32.567/35.756 ms. The 20 ms median is real CPU cost, but it is not the cause of the 464 ms or 3,558 ms synchronous tails. Moving the existing B9 assembly with the collector keeps it away from interaction/paint without inventing a new dependency graph.

Retain the existing memo rules, `readHead` optimization, and compatible committed-node refresh path. No-change work must not reassemble B9 or resend the full view. A broad topology/status split, new invalidation schema or batch-head API is deferred until a measured remaining bottleneck requires it. Reopen that decision if the actual worker version misses the CPU/memory/progress budgets or B9’s own share; do not pre-approve an ownership expansion now. IPC/adoption cost is unmeasured and must be demonstrated, not subtracted from the old table as if already known.

4. **B12, B12M, and B10C ownership**

**No B12 publication or lease API change is required for this route.** B10C instantiates and consumes the existing APIs in the worker that owns them. It must not transfer authority or prepared identities through messages. No change to checkpoint format, readerVersion, observation schema, fencing, lease generation/TTL, B16 continuity/notice logic or ledger writes follows from the execution move.

**B12M remains a separate prerequisite for complete recovery-budget acceptance.** The inspected `readRecovery` fetches projection/blob/identity before checking blob length, returns no complete read statistics, and its lazy `RecoveryIndexLookup.get/page` has no shared per-grant byte/time meter (`observation-store.ts:297–409`). Recovery lookups can also be supplied after publication ACK and used by later reader calls. Running them in a worker does not make them free or bounded.

B12M owns the additive budget/metering handoff and its tests: admission before oversized recovery fetches, pinned-commit identity, bounded lazy key/page lookup, actual accounting including failure/continuation, and charging each lazy use to the currently active collection grant. State precisely whether bytes denote logical decoded payload or actual storage I/O; do not claim physical SQLite I/O from JSON lengths. B10C consumes that handoff inside its worker and combines it once with native-reader charges, without double-counting the current inline reservation. Leave B12 source files to their owner.

Reject option 2 as a prerequisite and as an addition to B12M. Combining renewal and publication may save one eligible FULL transaction, but acquisition/recovery still precede capture, and one remaining commit can still stall. It does not remove the need for isolation or prove the maximum gate. If later evidence justifies it, commission a separately scoped B12 publication optimization, coordinating B16’s returned lease/expiry state and preserving the same-instance fence and atomic ACK. Do not hide it inside a package described as metered recovery reads.

In-scope B10C implementation after approval is the worker entry/client and bounded DTO under owned collector/link paths, scheduler/lifecycle wiring in bar/panel, pure freshness reuse, resource/latency telemetry and focused tests. Retain the default read-only collector behavior and the accepted M1/native/provenance limitations. Changes to renderer design, B17 collectors, B16 semantics, B12 internals or a shared service require their own owners; this decision does not authorize those edits.

5. **Acceptance required from B10C and B14**

B10C must first provide a local before/after table with **at least 1,000 measured operations per declared local population**, real bar and panel renderers, 200 visible nodes and the 20,000-header fixture. Show all three timings above alongside the old full-operation interval, stage timings, bytes, queue delay, message size, memory and CPU. Name the exact code/profile. The existing measurements qualify neither the proposed worker nor its IPC/resource budgets.

Measure bar and panel separately and concurrently. At minimum, keep these populations distinct: genuine no-change/no-publication; summary-only publication; new-checkpoint publication; and the existing mixed changing case of one transcript append plus 199 changing managed heartbeat rows. Record actual attempt/commit classes rather than mixing zero-attempt ticks into publication-only percentiles. Include ordinary acquire/renew/expiry opportunities at real elapsed times, followers, deadline edges and recovery continuation. Do not reset the lease clock or strip freshness work to obtain a favorable no-op series. A separately drained true no-change series is useful, but the natural mixed series is also required.

Required failure and boundary tests are:

- **Real process contention and ownership:** bar + panel on the same session, one accepted publisher, follower adoption, graceful owner exit, SIGKILL/worker failure, expiry takeover and late old-generation rejection. An unexpired lease is not authority for a replacement worker/store. Show no worker/service orphan after repeated open/close and parent exit.
- **Storage stalls and UI independence:** deterministically hold/slow acquisition, renewal and `publishPrepared` independently, including a several-second stall corresponding to the observed failure. Exercise resize, keyboard interaction, clocks and repaint during it. Paint must satisfy its gate; the delayed observation must be recorded as a progress-gate failure with visible health/freshness, not called an overall pass. Do not launch a second writer to bypass it.
- **Commit/ACK/IPC cut points:** fault before COMMIT, during publication, after COMMIT before the result, and after message send before adoption acknowledgement. Reopen and reconcile the store. Expose only complete commits; no pre-commit summary/metrics/ACK, duplicate logical publish, lost accepted notice baseline, or guessed rollback. Backpressure must not drop committed outcomes/notices or grow a queue.
- **Context and display correctness:** stale worker incarnation/request, root/run/binding changes during an operation, database/inode/source-generation replacement, delayed or duplicated result, missing result and unavailable store. Adopt only compatible committed state; preserve original stamps and clock provenance. Show stale transitions while the worker is stuck and recovery only from an actual later successful read.
- **Budgets and fairness:** 200-node burst, maximum legal record including LF/signature cost, recovery larger than the remaining grant, lazy-index reads after ACK, thrown reads, and cache eviction/recovery. All shared grants remain charged once; each eligible session makes fair progress; no endless reconstruct-and-yield loop. Zero age-only commits, zero no-change source bytes, zero extra redraw/status/wait writes.
- **Resource and lifecycle limits:** actual message sizes/queue depth, worker count, JS/external memory and whole-process RSS; simultaneous bar/panel, repeated scope changes and at least 30 minutes of operation. Exercise memory-limit failure, failed startup, missing hints, owner shutdown during a native stall, and the bounded restart policy. Confirm no unbounded cache growth and account for SQLite close/checkpoint work.

Normal supported profiles must satisfy the numeric paint, progress, propagation, stage and resource gates, not just the injected-failure responsiveness test. Preserve all failing attempts. If the bar’s multi-second lease stall persists, return a failed progress qualification even when paint is fast; do not silently widen the one-second bound.

Formal B14 qualification still needs **at least 10,000 consecutive samples per declared supported profile/entry/publication population, across at least three fresh starts with at least 1,000 samples from each**, the complete rolling-window checks, and the seven existing B14 evidence obligations adapted to the explicit new timing boundaries. Preserve maximum-size/history and cold/catch-up populations, WAL/checkpoint progress under concurrent readers including the pinned-reader negative case, exact Node/SQLite/platform/storage settings, control-plane F08 tests under observation load, startup/shutdown evidence, and all failed/incomplete runs. No worker result or local 1,000-sample comparison waives those obligations. Recovery-byte qualification remains conditional on the B12M handoff.
