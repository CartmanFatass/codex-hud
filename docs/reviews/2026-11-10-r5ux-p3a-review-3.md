## Findings

### 1. Settlement callbacks can revoke consent before successful text is released, without another publication fence

**`src/collectors/small-model.ts:1423–1433`**, with **`:1441`** — `job.resolve()` marks the job settled before calling telemetry and lifecycle observers, so revocation inside those callbacks is ignored by the authority subscriber and the original successful result is subsequently released. **Severity: major.**

Concrete scenario: keep the returned job handle and use the supported synchronous `onChange` observer to call `authority.setConsent(recipientId, 'deny', ...)` when `handle.status` becomes `settled`. The job-only microtask guarantees that the caller has the handle before this callback. On completion:

1. The worker's last authorization check succeeds.
2. `job.resolve(success)` sets `job.settled = true`.
3. `setStatus(job, 'settled')` invokes `onChange`; it durably revokes consent and notifies subscribers.
4. The job's revocation subscriber immediately returns because `job.settled` is already true.
5. `resolve(value)` releases the original success and its text without revalidation.

This also applies to a worker cache hit: cached text can be returned after the callback has revoked its grant. Invalidating path snapshots in `callback()` does not help because there is no subsequent publication check. The existing queued/running/compose and pre-microtask revocation tests do not exercise settlement callbacks. This violates the required revocation/publication fence, independently of whether a consumer later asks the fail-closed `jobMaterials` getter.

**Suggested fix:** Add a job-only, fresh authorization fence after all pre-publication callbacks and before resolving successful text, preserving typed pending and cancellation semantics. Add real transport-result and cache-hit regressions that revoke from settlement/telemetry callbacks and assert no successful text is released.

### 2. Budget exhaustion at the first post-reservation check strands the persistent claim

**`src/collectors/small-model.ts:1716`**, with **`:1729`** and **`:1672–1679`** — The persistent reservation is not recorded in `job.reserved` until after a budget-throwing authorization check, so pending at that check skips claim cleanup and is incorrectly classified as pre-reservation. **Severity: major.**

Concrete scenario: the real `store.reserve()` successfully writes a call charge, `claims.json` entry and unique `.active` marker, then the fixed dispatch budget is exhausted at the immediately following `this.authorized(job)`. This can be forced through the budget-consuming clock callback that runs inside the real reservation, or occur when fresh post-reservation evidence requires the remaining probes.

`JobBudgetPending` bypasses the boolean-false cleanup branch at lines 1716–1718. `run()` catches it and calls `pendingDispatch(job)`, but `job.reserved` is still unset. That method increments `pendingBeforeReserve`, never calls `store.finish()`, and returns pending. The worker then releases its local slot while leaving the persistent claim and active marker behind. A same-digest retry sees a live owner and marker, so `ModelRuntimeStore.reserve()` repeatedly returns `waiting` instead of progressing while this panel remains alive. The persisted charge is also misreported as pre-reservation pending, contrary to q9.

The existing post-reservation regression exhausts the budget from the **running** callback, which occurs only after line 1729; it therefore cannot catch this earlier window.

**Suggested fix:** Record `reservation.kind === 'reserved'` immediately after `reserve()` returns, before any throwing validation/callback, and ensure every pending/exception exit after reservation releases the claim while retaining its charge. Add a regression exhausting the budget before `markRunning()` and check claim/marker removal, `pendingAfterReserve`, and successful same-input retry.
