## Findings

### 1. Major — an ordinary helper receipt now crashes every watcher scan

**Location:** `packages/hud-mcp/bin/hud-watch.mjs:108–109,152`.

`sessionFolders()` appends `/sessions` to every `.dispatch` entry, including regular files, then calls `lstatSync`. `throwIfNoEntry: false` does not suppress **ENOTDIR**. The new unconditional call from `scan()` is outside the preservation routine's catch, so this exception terminates the watcher.

**Owner-visible scenario:** a worker uses a sonic/review helper. The installed pi-roles' `bin/pi-role-call:173–179` writes the normal `.dispatch/current.json` receipt. On the next scan or startup, the watcher tries to lstat `.dispatch/current.json/sessions` and exits 1. Finished tabs stop being reaped and retention stops; restarting the watcher meets the same receipt and crashes again. This needs neither a malformed tree nor a race.

**Evidence:** an independent temporary-home probe with an expired job, `.dispatch/sonic-1/sessions/` and `.dispatch/current.json` exited **1** with `ENOTDIR ... lstat '.../.dispatch/current.json/sessions'`. The job remained and `watch.log` contained only `start`. The helper producer was inspected read-only, not run. Existing helper-layout tests create only directories, omitting this normal receipt.

**Smallest fix:** enumerate `.dispatch` with directory entries and select real directories before constructing their `sessions` paths; keep the existing final-folder symlink exclusion. Include `current.json` in an existing helper-layout regression fixture.

### 2. Major — degraded watching goes dormant during a failed tab's retention wait and misses subsequent jobs

**Location:** `packages/hud-mcp/bin/hud-watch.mjs:197–199,218–219`.

A failed job awaiting its reap deadline sets `nearest` but leaves `open` false. With no filesystem watcher, this bypasses the one-second fallback and leaves the process alive with only the failed-job deadline timer.

**Owner-visible scenario:** filesystem watching is unavailable and a failed tab is being retained for the default two hours. The owner starts another job during that wait. `ensureWatcher()` (`packages/hud-mcp/src/thin-main.ts:195–203`) sees the still-live watcher PID and starts no replacement, but that watcher no longer polls or receives file events. The new job can finish and its tab stays open until the old failed-job deadline. This is a deterministic gap in the explicitly supported degraded mode, not a timing race.

**Evidence:** an independent real-watcher probe injected permanent `fs.watch` failure, initially supplied one newly failed tabbed job, then created and settled a second tabbed job. Instrumentation delegated to the real directory reader and timers. Output was exactly one scan and `timer 7199950`; after more than two fallback intervals the recorded watcher PID was still alive, the second job had no `reaped_at`, and the synthetic reaper had never run.

**Smallest fix:** retain the short degraded-mode scan while either active jobs **or pending failed-tab deadlines** keep the watcher alive, e.g. gate it on `(open || nearest !== Infinity) && !watcher`. Once neither remains, keep the current immediate exit.
