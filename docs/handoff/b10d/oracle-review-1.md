1. **P1 — Eviction can lose a real ledger update that has not been published.**

   Location: `src/collectors/delegation.ts:264–267`, with eviction at `203–211` and the publication skip at `333`.

   `observeLedger()` advances `reading.demandToken` for every reading, including a resident reader whose publisher still represents the previous ledger facts. In the same operation, admission of another session can evict that reader: its checkpoint is accepted and `hasPending()` is false, even though its changed ledger facts have not yet reached `publishOne()`. Eviction removes `hasReading()`, leaves a caught-up/clean reading, and the latest token is already recorded. With no subsequent native/ledger change, neither `pending()` nor `publishOne()` revisits it.

   Concrete case: warm more than 16 sessions; change resident A's run to a final terminal/uncertain state while a dormant B becomes selected for capture. Make A the oldest evictable resident. B's admission evicts A before A's new facts are published. After the final ledger update stops, A's durable status can remain permanently on its old state.

   In-scope fix: distinguish observed relevance from the relevance incorporated into an accepted publication. Carry an actual outstanding relevance change across eviction, or exclude a reader with that outstanding change from quiescent eviction until its publication is settled. Advance the accepted token only at the corresponding successful accepted boundary. Do not dirty every eviction or every global ledger high-water change. Add a deterministic real-store regression for the update-before-eviction ordering, with no further updates to rescue it.

2. **P1 — Same-root scope churn never retires obsolete collection state.**

   Location: `src/collectors/delegation.ts:133`, `574–587`, `656–660`, and `942–952`; scheduling membership is limited in `src/core/link/tick.ts:382–390`.

   `readings`, its retained envelope/raw/topology state, and associated continuation bookkeeping are removed only by closing the entire collector. `worker-entry.ts:80` recreates that collector only when the root string changes. A link or native child can leave the current bounded tree while the root remains unchanged, but its state remains resident indefinitely and still participates in slot accounting and store-head work. Repeated membership changes therefore grow retained session metadata beyond the 200-node scope.

   More seriously, after the complete root itself has been evicted, 16 children with accepted fixed-EOF partial tails can occupy all resident slots. If those children then leave the projected scope, they will no longer receive grants to finish their tails. New in-scope readers can be refused forever by those obsolete pins. An unpublished candidate that disappears from the view also cannot be found by `publishOne()`'s current-view loop, and can subsequently prevent `settlePending()` from completing a root transition.

   In-scope fix: add explicit bounded retirement driven by the actual retained scope, with a settled treatment of pending candidates/outcomes and the existing authority lifecycle. Remove obsolete envelopes, recovery/runtime references and auxiliary entries once retirement is legitimate. Do not solve this by discarding tails of still-in-scope readers, forgetting accepted authority during ordinary cache eviction, increasing 16/200, or silently deleting pending evidence. Follow the requested retirement consultation for the exact settle/retire ordering. Test same-root removal/replacement with both ordinary accepted readers and pinned tails, plus a pending-publication removal.

5. **P2 — The codec accepts malformed DTOs that can poison the adopted base and stop the result stream.**

6. **P2 — Rejected transport outcomes are recorded as completed commit-to-paint measurements.**

   Location: `src/core/link/worker-client.ts:419–420`, `482–510`; `tests/int/b10d-performance.mjs:87–90`.

   The rejection path calls `consume('rejected')` without painting. `consume()` still assigns finite commit-to-paint lower/upper values whenever the result has a commit trace, and the performance harness includes every non-null value. A real successful publication followed by `worker_payload_limit` therefore produces an apparently measured commit-to-paint interval even though the committed view was never delivered. An old-scope commit settled during a scope transition can similarly be paired with a null/new-scope frame.

   In-scope fix: distinguish consumption/rejection time from paint enqueue. Emit a completed commit-to-paint sample only when a compatible painted view actually contains that commit or a compatible newer one; retain the accepted commit identity and incomplete/undelivered outcome otherwise. Do not simply discard all continued/failed rows: a painted local commit may be valid even if another part of the operation is incomplete. Add the commit-then-oversize/rejection case and make qualification aggregation reject missing required progress instead of accepting these finite pseudo-paint timings. Keep the approved conservative COMMIT interval and clock uncertainty.

7. **P2 — Scope invalidation and close drop admitted/incomplete operation accounting.**

   Location: `src/core/link/worker-client.ts:268–273`, `285–287`, `180–215`, and `572–600`.

   If scope changes during an admitted asynchronous legacy read, `invalidateScope()` clears the held grant and repurposes its request as a new-scope pending request. The old read's later `submitLegacy()` is rejected, losing the work already performed; the later result records a zero-root activation and a superseded count without those root charges or a terminal superseded/incomplete record. On close, pending/root-grant state is cleared, subsequent results are ignored, and the closed exit path skips the active-operation record. A known unconsumed commit can likewise disappear from final operation accounting. This does not prove rollback and must not be presented as one.

   In-scope fix: emit bounded final accounting for the superseded admitted root slice and for shutdown-cancelled pending, active and unconsumed work, preserving oldest enqueue age, known charges and known/unknown commit outcome. Use conservative charging when actual root statistics are unavailable, without double charging a later callback. Do not dispatch another read or add a queue to collect this accounting. Test invalidation mid-root-read and close at root-grant, worker-active, pending and unconsumed-result stages.

8. **P2 — Busy/unavailable-worker ticks manufacture successful legacy read times.**

   Location: `src/index.ts:215–236`; `src/tree-page.ts:943–945` and `980–981`.

   A cadence without a root grant deliberately uses cached legacy data without even checking the rollout. Both entries then call `health.read()`, whose defined meaning is a successful session/rollout read or verified unchanged file. After the one replacement has failed, the entries can keep advancing “Last successful read” every cadence indefinitely even though the parser never reads again. The stale cached values are also built with `stale=false` unless another operation throws.

   In-scope fix: advance the legacy successful-read clock only after an actual successful permitted read or an actual compatible unchanged-file validation. Preserve the previous clock on a cached-only busy/unavailable opportunity, and expose the worker/collection limitation through the existing health/coverage wiring. Do not turn message receipt or a repaint into a successful source observation. Test this with a changed rollout while busy and after permanent worker unavailability.
