## Oracle recommendation

**Recommendation: include pre-lock preparation in the existing 30 ms authorization-and-transaction budget. Do not restart the clock at `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`, and do not grant preparation an additional budget.**

Status: recommendation for the owner, not an implementation acceptance or an approved contract amendment.

Reviewed `universal-hud` at commit [`f1e2a8408fbc6ddef181978e8a2d9fd5831242e9`](https://github.com/CartmanFatass/codex-hud/commit/f1e2a8408fbc6ddef181978e8a2d9fd5831242e9). This consultation was read-only. No files were written, tests executed, or local-only work attempted.

### 1. Recommended boundary

For the MCP write/authorization paths governed by P-06-5:

- Start one monotonic deadline before per-call source resolution and authorization preparation. The existing `tools/call` timestamp is an appropriate conservative boundary.
- Include per-call process/source evidence, file reads and stats, brief/config snapshots, hashes and normalization, capability preparation, queued ledger work, authorization transactions, subsequent intent/handshake/report/cancel transactions, retries and backoff.
- End the authorization-and-transaction measurement after the relevant final commit, rollback, or refusal decision. Record any additional response preparation and serialization under the full MCP response measurement.
- Never pause or reset this deadline across an `await`, between preparation and locking, or between successive transactions.
- Keep single `busy_timeout ≤10 ms` and cumulative lock waits/retries/backoff `≤20 ms`. These are nested limits within the same 30 ms, not additional allowances.
- Preparation-stage targets may be useful engineering allocations, but must fit inside the existing total.

The required Claude hook that runs before MCP receipt belongs to the same-call joint `<150 ms` measurement. It is not newly placed inside a 30 ms clock starting before the hook. MCP-internal `<50 ms` remains measured from complete `tools/call` to response serialization/flush. `delegate_wait` retains its existing wait budget and separate upfront-cost reporting. This ruling does not add a write transaction to status or wait.

### 2. Why this reading

The [F08 ruling](https://github.com/CartmanFatass/codex-hud/blob/f1e2a8408fbc6ddef181978e8a2d9fd5831242e9/docs/reviews/2026-11-01-chatgpt-pro-f08-hook-budget.md) §3 specifies total authorization-and-transaction wall clock, including wait and commit. It does not describe a lock-holding-only budget.

Contract 06 P-06-4 explicitly includes validation/digest preparation in MCP cost and forbids moving upfront work outside the measured cost. P-06-5 separately requires external preparation outside the write transaction. **Outside the transaction does not mean outside the budget.** Contract 02, lines 76–78, makes the same distinction and forbids moving filesystem or `/proc` evidence into the transaction to fix races.

The alternative readings would therefore need a substantive owner-approved relaxation:

- **Start at lock acquisition:** excludes both preparation and initial lock acquisition delay from the stated total, weakening its purpose.
- **Separate additive preparation budget:** increases authorized total work even if the full 50 ms MCP gate remains. No binding source currently grants it.

P-06-4 alone does not settle every detail of the 30 ms boundary. The proposed text below makes that boundary explicit while preserving F08’s limits.

### 4. Required code work after owner approval

Do not “fix” `delegation.ts:417` by moving the start time or increasing the production deadline.

Instead, use one shared per-call budget through the applicable source, authorization, preparation, and ledger paths:

1. **Carry the original absolute deadline into the final transaction.**  
   `acceptIntent` currently receives a relative lock allowance, normalizes/hashes at `src/ledger/intents.ts:197–201`, and submits the transaction without the original authorization deadline. Its callback does not check that deadline. `Ledger.transaction` similarly controls acquisition/queue allowance, not total callback-and-commit duration. A check only at `delegation.ts:417` cannot account for work or scheduling after that check.

2. **Check before admitting further work and before committing an unaccepted operation.**  
   Recheck after queued waits and preparation. When the budget is already exhausted, do not start another acceptance transaction. If expiration is detected before commit, roll back the requested operation. Preserve the existing scope, generation, owner, capability, slot, and expected-run checks.

3. **Preserve committed truth.**  
   Synchronous SQLite commit cannot be forcibly interrupted by a JavaScript timer. A commit that returns after the budget is a recorded performance defect, not permission to report an unaccepted operation. Return/recover the committed result and never dispatch a duplicate. Keep the existing post-acceptance protections in spawn and cancel.

4. **Account for cumulative waiting across transactions.**  
   Root authorization, final acceptance, retries, reconciliation, and optional rejection auditing must not each receive a fresh 20 ms. Current elapsed-time deductions are conservative but are not measurements of actual lock waiting. Keep conservative charging until a reliable split exists; report preparation, connection queue, lock/backoff, transaction body, and commit separately.

5. **Measure the local entry consistently.**  
   `resumeLocal`, at `delegation.ts:490–503`, currently constructs and checks `PrivateLedgerGate` before starting its 30 ms clock. If local resume continues to use this same acceptance budget, its per-call authorization work must be included rather than silently excluded.

6. **Resolve the existing local-gate conflict explicitly.**  
   `acceptIntent:204–207` calls `localCheck` inside the write transaction. `PrivateLedgerGate.check()` performs `realpathSync`/`lstatSync`, contrary to the external-I/O prohibition. Do not simply delete those security checks. The follow-on design must preserve store identity/replacement protection while meeting the transaction boundary, or return a narrowly described owner question if it cannot.

Keep external `busy` compatibility. Internal diagnostics may distinguish preparation expiry, queued expiry, lock contention, and late commit without adding secrets or a new public failure taxonomy.

### 5. Tests and acceptance

Separate deterministic correctness coverage from real-clock qualification.

**Add deterministic budget tests**, using a trusted internal clock/preparation seam, never a tool argument or production configuration knob:

- Slow source resolution exhausts the original deadline.
- Slow brief/config/stat preparation exhausts it despite an uncontended ledger.
- Preparation consumes part of the budget and leaves only the true remainder for authorization and acceptance.
- Multiple transactions and backoffs share the cumulative limits.
- A zero remaining budget cannot become an unlimited queued attempt. Preserve intentionally nonblocking low-level transactions separately.
- Expiration after preparation but before the transaction callback prevents a new accepted operation.
- Expiration after root authorization or death reconciliation prevents later acceptance. Earlier legitimate identity/reconciliation bookkeeping is not incorrectly asserted to be zero.
- A slow successful commit remains accepted, is reported as over budget, and replays without another launch.
- A stale source/generation after preparation is refused without refreshing its authority.

Keep the existing B6 test at `tests/unit/b6-cancel.test.ts:156–190`, which verifies no late cancellation transaction after reconciliation consumes the budget.

For the listed semantic suites, a controlled internal time dependency can prevent unrelated host scheduling from replacing the business outcome being tested. Retain separate production-clock tests. Do not weaken assertions to accept `busy` everywhere, move request starts into the future, suppress failures, or raise the production limit.

**Retain and extend performance evidence:**

- The B45 direct-service and framed-stdio benchmarks already preserve uncontended-busy attempts and report over-30/every-sample flags. Keep those features.
- `tests/hud-mcp/handshake-benchmark.test.ts:115–118` checks only p95 for the 30 ms upper bound. That alone cannot establish compliance with the per-call limit.
- Report all outcomes and over-budget samples, including commit overruns. Do not substitute transaction-only measurements for full authorization.
- Use the contract’s 50 warm-ups, ≥1,000 calls per non-wait tool/combination, H/R=1/1 and 2/10, heartbeat/lock contention, and separate success/refusal/busy distributions. Report n, nearest-rank p50/p95/p99/max, host, command, and at least three remote runs.
- Preserve cold/cache-first-use evidence and concurrent observation-load checks.
- Real Claude joint qualification, real harness coverage, and formal supported-platform acceptance remain local-only/B14. Synthetic passes do not promote capability cells.

Known-flake reruns may still follow the current workflow, with both outputs recorded. They do not establish that P-06-5 passes or authorize changing its meaning.

### 6. Contract and ownership impact

**Yes, contract text should be amended, but as an explicit scope clarification with unchanged thresholds.** Update P-06-5, T-06-14, and the measurement protocol; add a decision-log entry and a matching cross-reference in contract 05. Preserve the historical F08 ruling.

Suggested normative addition:

> P-06-5 的 30 ms 是单次调用的授权与事务总墙钟，不是持锁时长。MCP 从完整 tools/call 进入逐调用验证、来源解析与授权准备之前开始计时，包含本次文件读取/stat、进程与来源取证、参数与摘要准备、连接队列等待，以及全部相关授权/提交事务的等锁、重试、退避和 commit；在最后相关事务提交、回滚或拒绝决定完成时结束。准备必须在写事务之外，但仍在同一预算之内；不得在取得写锁、完成准备或开始下一事务时重置时钟，也不另加准备预算。单次 busy_timeout≤10 ms、累计等锁/重试/退避≤20 ms，均包含在30 ms内。耗尽且目标操作尚未提交时不得再接受该操作；已提交结果不改报未接受，同步commit超时单列预算缺陷。MCP完整响应和显式Claude hook联合计时仍分别按P-06-4；wait的等待预算不变。
